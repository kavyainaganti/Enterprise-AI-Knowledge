# Document Control and Policy Management Procedure

## 1. Purpose

Employees, managers and the AI knowledge assistant all rely on company documents to make decisions. If documents are out of date, unapproved or contradict each other, people make wrong decisions. This procedure sets out how company policies, guidelines and procedures are written, identified, approved, published, reviewed and retired, and, most importantly, **which document prevails when two documents appear to conflict**. It is the reference for resolving contradictions.

## 2. Scope

The procedure covers all governing documents: policies, standards, guidelines, procedures, guides, frequently asked questions and templates that tell people what to do. It does not cover contracts, customer documentation or project deliverables, which have their own controls.

## 3. Document hierarchy

| Level | Type | Example | Typical approver |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Board-approved policies | Delegation of Authority (OPS-002), Anti-Bribery (CMP-001) | Board of Directors or a Board committee |
| 2 | Company policies | Leave and Holidays Policy (HR-POL-001) | CEO, CFO or CHRO as stated |
| 3 | Guidelines and standards | Identity, Password and Access Management (IT-GDL-002) | CIO or CISO |
| 4 | Procedures | Procurement and Purchase Order Procedure (OPS-001) | Function head |
| 5 | Guides, FAQs and templates | Manager's Onboarding Guide (ONB-002) | Function head |

Lower-level documents must be consistent with the higher-level documents they support. A procedure may add detail to a policy but may not change it.

## 4. Document identification and metadata

### 4.1 Document IDs

Every governing document has a **unique ID** made of a prefix and a number:

| Prefix | Area |
|---|---|
| HR-POL | Human resources policies |
| IT-GDL | IT guidelines |
| FIN-RMB | Finance reimbursement rules |
| ONB | Onboarding |
| CMP | Compliance |
| OPS | Operational procedures |
| CTR | Labelled contradiction examples used to test conflict detection (never authoritative) |

IDs are never reused, even after a document is retired.

### 4.2 Required metadata

Every document carries: ID, title, category, owning department, document type, version, **status**, effective date, review date, owner, approver, sensitivity label, allowed roles, related documents, and source reference. The **status** is one of:
- **approved** - the current authoritative version;
- **draft** - under preparation; not authoritative;
- **superseded** - replaced by a newer version; kept for reference only;
- **unofficial** - material that was never approved, such as a team wiki page or a local memo; not authoritative.

### 4.3 Versions

Versions use the format *major.minor*. A **major** change (new rules, changed limits or responsibilities) increases the major number and requires full approval. A **minor** change (clarifications, corrected references, formatting) increases the minor number and may be approved by the owner alone. Every document includes a revision history.

## 5. Creating and changing documents

1. The owner drafts the document using the standard template, marked **draft**.
2. The owner consults affected functions; Legal and Compliance reviews any document with legal or regulatory content; Security reviews documents affecting information security.
3. The owner checks the draft for conflicts with existing documents, using the policy library search and the AI knowledge assistant's conflict check.
4. The approver named in section 3 approves the document.
5. The knowledge management team publishes it in the **Policy Library**, sets the status to **approved**, and marks the previous version **superseded**.
6. A summary of major changes is announced in TeamTalk and, for policies affecting all employees, by email.

## 6. Review cycle

Every document is reviewed at least **every 12 months**. Low-risk guides and FAQs may be reviewed every **24 months** with the knowledge management team's agreement. Documents are also reviewed whenever a law, system or organisational change affects them. A document past its review date remains in force but is flagged in the Policy Library and reported to the owner's department head.

## 7. Which document prevails: precedence rules

When two documents appear to give different answers, apply these rules **in order**:

1. **Law prevails.** If a document conflicts with applicable law, the law applies, and the document must be corrected.
2. **Only approved documents count.** Documents with the status **draft**, **superseded** or **unofficial** are **never authoritative**, whatever they say and however recent they are. The same applies to emails, chat messages, presentations and memos that have not been published as approved documents.
3. **Higher level prevails.** Between two approved documents at different levels of the hierarchy, the higher-level document prevails (for example the Delegation of Authority over a procedure).
4. **Specific prevails over general at the same level.** Between two approved documents at the same level, the one written specifically for the topic prevails (for example the Business Travel Policy over the general Expense Reimbursement Policy for a travel question).
5. **Later effective date prevails.** If the documents are at the same level and equally specific, the one with the later effective date applies.

### 7.1 Regional addenda and memos

Local or regional variations (for example an office-specific rule) are valid **only** if published as an **approved addendum** by the approver of the main policy. A memo from a local manager cannot change a company policy, even if it was circulated widely. Employees who receive such a memo should follow the approved policy and report the conflict.

### 7.2 Reporting and fixing conflicts

Anyone who finds a conflict reports it to the owner of the higher-level or more recent document through HelpPoint under *Policy Conflict*. The owner acknowledges within **5 working days** and resolves the conflict **within 30 days**, by correcting, superseding or withdrawing the conflicting document. Until it is resolved, the precedence rules above decide which rule applies.

## 8. The Policy Library and the AI knowledge assistant

The **Policy Library** is the single source of truth for governing documents. The AI knowledge assistant indexes the Policy Library and:
- answers from **approved** documents by default;
- may retrieve superseded, draft or unofficial documents only to explain history or to point out a conflict, and must label them clearly as not authoritative;
- applies the precedence rules in section 7 when sources disagree, and cites both sources;
- respects each document's allowed roles (IT-GDL-005).

The library also contains documents with the prefix **CTR**, which are deliberately conflicting examples used to test conflict detection. They are labelled as contradiction examples and are never authoritative.

## 9. Retirement and retention

When a document is withdrawn or replaced, its status becomes **superseded** and it is removed from default search results. Superseded documents are retained for **7 years** after supersession, as required by the Data Classification, Handling and Retention Guideline (IT-GDL-004), so that past decisions can be understood.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Document owner | Draft, maintain and review the document; resolve conflicts within 30 days |
| Approver | Approve new and changed documents within their authority |
| Legal and Compliance | Own this procedure; review legal content; maintain the hierarchy |
| Knowledge management team | Publish documents, manage metadata and status, run the Policy Library and assistant indexing |
| All employees | Use only approved documents; report conflicts and out-of-date content |

## 11. Exceptions

Urgent changes required by law or a serious risk may be published by the approver with a shortened consultation, and must be reviewed fully within 60 days. No exception allows an unapproved document to be treated as authoritative.

## 12. Worked examples

**Example 1 - old leave policy.** An employee finds the superseded Leave Policy version 3.0, which gives 15 days of earned leave. The current approved Leave and Holidays Policy (HR-POL-001, version 4.1) gives 18 days. Rule 2 applies: superseded documents are not authoritative, so the entitlement is 18 days.

**Example 2 - regional memo.** A Pune office memo says hotels in metro cities can cost up to Rs 7,500 per night. The approved Business Travel Policy sets Rs 6,000. Under section 7.1 the memo cannot change company policy, so the limit is Rs 6,000.

**Example 3 - specific over general.** For a question about international meal allowances, the Business Travel Policy (FIN-RMB-002) applies rather than the general Expense Reimbursement Policy (FIN-RMB-001), because it is more specific to travel.

**Example 4 - higher level.** A procedure states an approval limit that differs from the Delegation of Authority. The Delegation of Authority, a Board-approved policy, prevails.

**Example 5 - draft procurement note.** A draft note proposes three quotes above Rs 1,00,000. Drafts are not authoritative; the approved Procurement procedure (three quotes above Rs 2,00,000) applies.

## 13. Frequently asked questions

**Q: Which document should I follow if two documents disagree?**
A: Apply the precedence rules: law first, then only approved documents, then higher level, then more specific, then later effective date.

**Q: Is a draft or superseded document ever authoritative?**
A: No.

**Q: Can a local manager's memo change a company policy?**
A: No. Only an approved addendum published by the policy's approver can create a local variation.

**Q: How often are documents reviewed?**
A: At least every 12 months; low-risk guides and FAQs every 24 months.

**Q: How quickly are reported conflicts fixed?**
A: Acknowledged within 5 working days and resolved within 30 days.

**Q: What are CTR documents?**
A: Labelled contradiction examples used to test conflict detection; they are never authoritative.

**Q: How long are superseded documents kept?**
A: 7 years after supersession.
