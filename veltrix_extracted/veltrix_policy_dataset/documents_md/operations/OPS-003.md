# Business Continuity and Crisis Management Procedure

## 1. Purpose

Floods, power failures, cyber-attacks, pandemics and the loss of a key supplier can all disrupt Veltrix's ability to serve customers and support employees. This procedure explains how the company prepares for such events, how it decides that a situation is a crisis, who leads the response, how critical activities are kept running or restored within agreed times, and how the company communicates with employees, customers and the public. It is restricted to the functions that plan and lead the response; employee-facing emergency instructions are in OPS-004.

## 2. Scope

The procedure covers all offices, remote work arrangements, company systems and the Veltrix products that customers depend on. It covers events affecting people (illness, injury, safety threats), places (office unavailable), technology (major outage or cyber-attack) and suppliers (failure of a critical vendor).

## 3. Definitions

- **Business continuity:** the ability to continue delivering critical activities at an acceptable level during a disruption.
- **Crisis:** a situation that threatens the safety of people, the continuity of critical activities, or the company's reputation, and requires coordinated leadership beyond normal management.
- **Business impact analysis (BIA):** a structured assessment of how quickly each activity must be restored and what it depends on.
- **Recovery time objective (RTO):** the maximum acceptable time to restore an activity or system.
- **Recovery point objective (RPO):** the maximum acceptable loss of data, measured in time.

## 4. Governance

### 4.1 Crisis Management Team

The **Crisis Management Team (CMT)** leads the company's response to any declared crisis. Members:
- **CEO** (chair);
- CFO;
- CHRO;
- CIO;
- CISO;
- CMO (communications);
- Head of Operations (coordinator).
Each member has a named deputy. The Head of Legal and Compliance joins when legal or regulatory issues arise.

### 4.2 Business continuity coordinators

Each department names a business continuity coordinator who keeps the department's continuity plan current, participates in exercises and acts as the contact point during a disruption.

## 5. Business impact analysis and critical activities

The BIA is **reviewed every year** by the Head of Operations with each department. The current critical activities and recovery objectives are:

| Critical activity | RTO | RPO | Main dependencies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer production platforms (Veltrix Insight, Flow, Connect) | **8 hours** | **4 hours** | Cloud hosting, on-call engineering |
| Customer support | **4 hours** | Not applicable | Support tool, telephony, support staff |
| Identity, email and TeamTalk | 8 hours | 4 hours | Identity provider, cloud services |
| Payroll | **48 hours**, and salaries must be paid by the last working day of the month | 1 day | Payroll system, bank portal, Payroll team |
| Supplier payments | 5 working days | 1 day | Finance system, bank portal |
| Month-end close | Completed by working day 5, with up to 3 days' delay acceptable in a declared crisis | 1 day | Finance system, Finance team |

The IT recovery objectives match the Backup and Disaster Recovery standard applied by IT (RPO 4 hours, RTO 8 hours) and the backup retention in IT-GDL-004.

## 6. Preparation

- Each department maintains a continuity plan listing critical activities, minimum staff, alternative ways of working, key contacts and suppliers.
- Critical roles have at least one trained back-up person.
- Work can move to remote working or another office: all employees have laptops and VPN access, and the hybrid model (HR-POL-002) means remote working is routine.
- Critical suppliers (cloud hosting, payroll provider, bank, telephony) are reviewed annually for their own continuity arrangements.
- Contact details for all employees are kept up to date in PeopleHub; the emergency notification system draws on these records.

## 7. Detecting and declaring a crisis

### 7.1 Escalation

Any manager who becomes aware of a serious event (for example an office fire, a P1 incident lasting more than 4 hours, a SEV-1 security incident, a natural disaster affecting an office city, or a death or serious injury at work) informs the Head of Operations immediately. IT incidents follow IT-GDL-003 and are escalated to the CMT when they meet these criteria.

### 7.2 Declaration

The Head of Operations convenes the CMT, or its available members, **within 1 hour**. The CEO, or the CFO in the CEO's absence, decides whether to **declare a crisis**. Once declared, the CMT:
1. confirms the facts and the immediate risks to people;
2. sets priorities: people first, then customers, then other operations;
3. assigns workstreams (people and safety, technology, customers, communications, finance);
4. agrees the next meeting time, normally every 2 to 4 hours during the acute phase.

## 8. Protecting people

- **Emergency notification:** employees affected by an event receive a message through TeamTalk and SMS **within 30 minutes** of the CMT deciding it is needed, with clear instructions.
- **Safety check-in:** employees in an affected location confirm they are safe through the notification system; HR follows up with anyone who has not responded **within 2 hours**.
- **Travellers:** the Travel Desk uses the travel register (FIN-RMB-002) to identify and contact employees travelling in affected areas.
- **Support:** HR arranges medical help, counselling through the employee assistance programme, and flexible leave for affected employees.

## 9. Keeping critical activities running

Departments activate their continuity plans for the affected activities. Typical measures include moving work to remote working or to another office, switching support calls to another location, restoring systems from backups in a different cloud region, and using back-up suppliers. IT technical recovery follows IT-GDL-003 and the disaster recovery runbooks. Finance makes sure that payroll and essential supplier payments continue, using manual bank-portal payments if needed, approved under the Delegation of Authority.

## 10. Communication

| Audience | Responsibility | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| Employees | CHRO with Internal Communications | TeamTalk, email, SMS, manager briefings |
| Customers | Customer Success with the CMO | Status page, account managers, email |
| Media and public | **Only the CEO, the CMO or a named spokesperson** | Approved statements |
| Regulators and authorities | Head of Legal and Compliance, CISO for CERT-In matters | As required |

All external statements are approved by the CMT. Employees must not comment publicly or on social media about a crisis. Where personal data is affected, the Data Protection Officer leads notification under CMP-003.

## 11. Recovery and review

The CMT stands down when critical activities are restored and the risk to people has passed. A **post-crisis review is completed within 15 working days**, covering what happened, how the response worked, and improvements. Improvements are tracked to completion by the Head of Operations.

## 12. Testing and exercises

- Each department's continuity plan is **tested at least once a year**, through a desk-top exercise or a live test.
- The CMT runs **one crisis simulation every year**, rotating scenarios (for example ransomware, office closure, pandemic, cloud region outage).
- IT runs disaster recovery tests **twice a year**.
- Evacuation drills are run under OPS-004.
Results and improvement actions are reported to the CEO.

## 13. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| CEO | Chair the CMT; declare and stand down crises |
| Head of Operations | Maintain this procedure and the BIA; coordinate the CMT; run exercises |
| CHRO | People and safety workstream; employee communications |
| CIO and CISO | Technology recovery and security response |
| CFO | Financial continuity; emergency spend under the DoA |
| CMO | External and customer communications |
| Department coordinators | Maintain and test department plans |

## 14. Exceptions

During a declared crisis, the CMT may temporarily vary normal procedures, including approval workflows, where needed to protect people or critical services. Every variation is recorded in the crisis log and reviewed in the post-crisis review. Spend approvals above normal limits follow the emergency rule in the Delegation of Authority.

## 15. Worked examples

**Example 1 - flooding in Bengaluru.** Heavy rain closes roads around the Bengaluru office. The CMT declares a crisis for the location, notifies Bengaluru employees within 30 minutes to work from home, and HR completes safety check-ins within 2 hours.

**Example 2 - cloud region outage.** A cloud provider region hosting Veltrix Insight fails. IT restores the service in a secondary region within the 8-hour RTO, with data loss no greater than the 4-hour RPO. Customer Success updates the status page.

**Example 3 - payroll at risk.** A ransomware attack on 27 March disables the payroll system. Finance uses the bank portal and the last approved payroll file, adjusted manually, so that salaries are paid by 31 March.

**Example 4 - media enquiry.** A journalist contacts an engineer about an outage. The engineer politely declines and passes the request to the CMO.

## 16. Frequently asked questions

**Q: Who chairs the Crisis Management Team?**
A: The CEO; the CFO decides on declaration in the CEO's absence.

**Q: How quickly must the CMT meet after a serious event?**
A: Within 1 hour of the Head of Operations being informed.

**Q: How quickly are employees notified?**
A: Within 30 minutes of the CMT deciding a notification is needed.

**Q: What is the RTO for customer production platforms?**
A: 8 hours, with an RPO of 4 hours.

**Q: How often is the business impact analysis reviewed?**
A: Every year.

**Q: Who may speak to the media during a crisis?**
A: Only the CEO, the CMO or a named spokesperson.

**Q: When is the post-crisis review completed?**
A: Within 15 working days of standing down.
