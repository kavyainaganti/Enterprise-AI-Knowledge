# Whistleblower and Speak-Up Policy

## 1. Purpose

Wrongdoing is most often discovered by the people closest to it. Veltrix wants everyone to feel safe raising concerns about fraud, bribery, data misuse, safety risks and other serious problems, and confident that their concern will be taken seriously and that they will be protected. This policy describes what can be reported, the channels available (including anonymous ones), how reports are handled and investigated, and the protection given to people who speak up in good faith.

## 2. Scope

Anyone can use this policy: employees, directors, interns, contractors, vendors, customers and members of the public. It covers concerns about past, present or likely future wrongdoing connected with Veltrix.

## 3. What to report

Examples of concerns that should be raised under this policy:
- fraud, theft, embezzlement or falsification of financial records or expense claims;
- bribery or corruption, including facilitation payments (CMP-001);
- undeclared conflicts of interest or improper gifts (CMP-002);
- misuse or unauthorised disclosure of personal or confidential data (CMP-003);
- serious breaches of information security;
- risks to health and safety;
- violations of law or regulation;
- retaliation against someone who raised a concern;
- deliberate concealment of any of the above.

### 3.1 What this policy is not for

Personal workplace grievances, such as disagreements about workload, a performance rating or a manager's decision, are handled through the grievance process: raise them first with your manager; if not resolved within **10 working days**, raise them in PeopleHub under *Grievance*, and HR responds within **15 working days**. Complaints of sexual harassment go to the Internal Committee under the POSH Policy (CMP-005). Security incidents that need immediate action are reported through the IT hotline (IT-GDL-003). If you are unsure which route to use, use this policy; the Ethics Committee will route the concern correctly.

## 4. Ethics Committee

Concerns under this policy are overseen by the **Ethics Committee**, made up of:
- the **CFO** (Vikram Mehta);
- the **CHRO** (Priya Nair);
- an **independent director** of the Board (Dr. Kiran Bhatt), who chairs the Committee.

The Head of Legal and Compliance acts as secretary. If a concern involves a member of the Ethics Committee, that member is excluded, and the concern is sent directly to the **chair of the Audit Committee**.

## 5. How to raise a concern

| Channel | Details |
|---|---|
| **Ethics hotline** | Telephone and web reporting service operated by an independent provider, available 24x7 in English, Hindi, Telugu, Kannada and Marathi; reports can be anonymous |
| Ethics mailbox | A dedicated email address read only by the Head of Legal and Compliance and the Ethics Committee chair |
| Direct contact | Any member of the Ethics Committee, or the Audit Committee chair for concerns about senior leaders |
| Your manager | Managers must pass any concern they receive to the ethics mailbox within 2 working days |

You may report **anonymously**. Anonymous reports are investigated, but it helps to provide enough detail (what, when, where, who, any evidence) and to check the hotline for follow-up questions using the reference number provided.

You do not need proof to raise a concern, only a reasonable belief that something is wrong. Do not investigate the matter yourself or gather evidence by accessing systems or data you are not authorised to use.

## 6. How concerns are handled

| Step | Timeline | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgement to the reporter | **Within 7 days** of receipt | Head of Legal and Compliance |
| Initial review: decide whether to investigate and who will investigate | **Within 30 days** | Ethics Committee |
| Investigation | Normally completed **within 90 days** of receipt | Appointed investigator |
| Outcome and actions | After investigation | Ethics Committee, with management |
| Feedback to the reporter | When the investigation closes | Head of Legal and Compliance |

### 6.1 Investigation

The investigator is independent of the people and area concerned and may be internal (for example Internal Audit) or an external specialist for complex fraud or senior-level cases. Investigations follow the principles of fairness used in HR-POL-005: people accused of wrongdoing are told the allegation and have a chance to respond, and findings are based on evidence.

### 6.2 Outcomes

Possible outcomes include disciplinary action under HR-POL-005, recovery of losses, changes to controls or processes, reporting to authorities, or a finding that the concern was not substantiated. The reporter is told that the investigation has closed and, where appropriate, the general outcome; details may be withheld to protect confidentiality and the rights of others.

## 7. Protection for people who speak up

- **No retaliation:** nobody may be dismissed, demoted, threatened, harassed, denied a promotion or otherwise disadvantaged for raising a concern in good faith, or for helping with an investigation. Retaliation is gross misconduct.
- **Confidentiality:** the identity of a reporter who gives their name is shared only with people who need to know to investigate, unless the reporter agrees otherwise or the law requires disclosure.
- **Good faith:** a reporter is protected even if the concern turns out to be mistaken, provided they genuinely believed it was true.
- **Support:** reporters who feel they have suffered retaliation should contact the Ethics Committee chair directly; the case will be treated as a new concern.

Knowingly making a false report to harm someone is misconduct and may lead to disciplinary action.

### 7.1 Concerns raised by vendors and customers

Vendors, partners and customers may use the ethics hotline or the ethics mailbox in the same way as employees; the channels are published on the company website and in vendor contracts. Their reports receive the same acknowledgement, review and investigation timelines. Veltrix will not end or downgrade a business relationship because a vendor or customer raised a concern in good faith.

### 7.2 Raising concerns outside the company

This policy does not prevent anyone from reporting a concern to a regulator, law enforcement agency or other authority where the law allows it, and nothing in an employment contract or settlement agreement may be used to stop such a report. Veltrix encourages people to use the internal channels first, because they allow the company to act quickly, but the protections in this section apply in either case.

## 8. Records and reporting

The Head of Legal and Compliance keeps a confidential register of all concerns, investigations and outcomes. Records are Restricted and are retained for **7 years** after the case closes. The Ethics Committee reports a summary of cases (without identifying reporters) to the Audit Committee every quarter, and the Audit Committee reviews the effectiveness of this policy every year.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Everyone | Raise genuine concerns; co-operate with investigations; keep matters confidential |
| Managers | Pass concerns to the ethics mailbox within 2 working days; never discourage reporting; prevent retaliation |
| Head of Legal and Compliance | Receive and acknowledge reports, maintain the register, coordinate investigations |
| Ethics Committee | Decide on investigations, oversee outcomes |
| Investigators | Investigate independently and fairly |
| Audit Committee | Oversee the policy; receive concerns about senior leaders and Ethics Committee members |

## 10. Exceptions

There are no exceptions to the protection against retaliation. Timelines in section 6 may be extended by the Ethics Committee for complex investigations, with the reporter informed of the new expected date.

## 11. Worked examples

**Example 1 - suspected expense fraud.** Pradeep notices that a colleague's travel claims include hotel nights when the colleague was in the office. He reports it anonymously through the ethics hotline and receives a reference number. He receives an acknowledgement within 7 days through the hotline portal.

**Example 2 - grievance.** Lavanya disagrees with her rating. This is a personal grievance, not a whistleblowing concern; she follows the performance appeal process or the grievance route.

**Example 3 - concern about a committee member.** An employee suspects the CFO approved a vendor owned by a relative. Because the CFO sits on the Ethics Committee, the employee sends the concern directly to the chair of the Audit Committee.

**Example 4 - manager receives a concern.** A team member tells her manager that a reseller asked for a cash payment. The manager forwards the concern to the ethics mailbox within 2 working days and does not investigate it personally.

**Example 5 - retaliation.** After raising a concern, a reporter is removed from a key project without explanation. The reporter contacts the Ethics Committee chair, and the possible retaliation is investigated as a new concern.

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**Q: Can I report anonymously?**
A: Yes, through the ethics hotline, which is available 24x7.

**Q: How quickly will I get an acknowledgement?**
A: Within 7 days of your report.

**Q: How long does an investigation take?**
A: Normally within 90 days of receipt, with an initial review within 30 days.

**Q: Who is on the Ethics Committee?**
A: The CFO, the CHRO and an independent director, who chairs it.

**Q: What if my concern is about a member of the Ethics Committee?**
A: Send it directly to the chair of the Audit Committee.

**Q: Will I be protected if I am wrong?**
A: Yes, provided you raised the concern in good faith.

**Q: Is a disagreement about my rating a whistleblowing matter?**
A: No. Use the grievance or performance appeal process.
