# Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) Policy

## 1. Purpose

Everyone at Veltrix has the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment. This policy states the company's commitment, defines sexual harassment, explains how to raise a complaint, describes how the Internal Committee inquires into complaints, and sets out the protections and support available. It is based on the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (the "POSH Act"). **Veltrix extends the same protection and process to employees of all genders**, beyond the minimum required by law.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to every Veltrix workplace, which includes:
- the Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Pune offices;
- customer and vendor sites where employees work;
- business travel, transport provided by the company, offsites and company events;
- remote working, including video calls, chat messages, email and social media when connected with work.

It protects employees, interns, contractors, trainees and visitors, and covers harassment by colleagues, managers, customers, vendors or any other person.

## 3. What is sexual harassment

Sexual harassment includes any unwelcome act or behaviour of a sexual nature, whether direct or implied, such as:
- physical contact and advances;
- a demand or request for sexual favours;
- sexually coloured remarks, jokes or comments about appearance;
- showing pornography or sexually explicit images;
- any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature, including messages, emojis, images or comments on chat and social media.

The following circumstances, among others, may also amount to sexual harassment when linked to such behaviour: an implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment, a threat of detrimental treatment, interference with work, or creating an intimidating, offensive or hostile environment.

What matters is the impact on the person receiving the behaviour, not the intention of the person doing it.

## 4. Internal Committee

### 4.1 Composition

An **Internal Committee (IC)** is constituted at each office. Each IC has:
- a **Presiding Officer**, who is a senior woman employee;
- **at least two employee members** committed to the cause of women or with experience in social work or law;
- **one external member** from a non-governmental organisation or an association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with sexual harassment issues.
At least half of the members are women. Members serve for up to 3 years. Their names and contact details are displayed in PeopleHub and on notice boards at each office.

### 4.2 Remote employees

Employees without a base office may approach the IC of the Hyderabad office or any IC member directly.

## 5. Making a complaint

- A complaint is made **in writing** to the IC (by email or letter, or through the confidential POSH form in PeopleHub). If the person cannot write, any IC member will help record it.
- A complaint should be made **within 3 months** of the incident, or of the last incident in a series. The IC may **extend this by a further 3 months** if there were reasons that prevented the complaint being made earlier.
- If the person affected is unable to complain because of physical or mental incapacity or death, a relative, friend, co-worker or other person permitted by law may complain on their behalf.
- Employees may also speak informally to an IC member for advice before deciding whether to make a formal complaint.

## 6. Process

### 6.1 Conciliation

Before starting an inquiry, and only at the request of the complainant, the IC may try to resolve the matter through conciliation. **No monetary settlement may be part of a conciliation.** If conciliation is agreed, the IC records the settlement and the matter is closed; if the terms are not followed, the complainant may ask the IC to proceed with an inquiry.

### 6.2 Inquiry

1. The IC sends a copy of the complaint to the respondent within 7 working days of receiving it.
2. The respondent submits a written reply, with any documents and names of witnesses, within 10 working days.
3. The IC hears both parties and witnesses separately, following the principles of natural justice. Lawyers do not represent the parties during the proceedings.
4. **The IC completes the inquiry within 90 days** of receiving the complaint.
5. The IC provides its **report and recommendations to the employer within 10 days** of completing the inquiry, with copies to both parties.
6. **The employer acts on the recommendations within 60 days** of receiving the report.

### 6.3 Interim relief

During the inquiry, on the complainant's written request, the IC may recommend interim measures such as transferring the complainant or the respondent to another team or office, granting the complainant **paid leave of up to 3 months** in addition to normal leave, or restraining the respondent from reporting on the complainant's work.

### 6.4 Outcomes

If the allegation is proved, the IC may recommend action including a written apology, a warning, withholding of promotion or increments, counselling, community service, termination, or a deduction from the respondent's salary as compensation to the complainant. Action against employees is implemented through the Employee Conduct and Disciplinary Policy (HR-POL-005). For third parties such as vendors or customers, the company takes action through the contract and may report the matter to the third party's employer.

### 6.5 Appeals

Either party may appeal against the IC's recommendations to the appellate authority provided under the applicable service rules or law, **within 90 days** of the recommendations.

## 7. Confidentiality and protection

The identity of the complainant, respondent and witnesses, the contents of the complaint, the inquiry proceedings and the recommendations are confidential and may not be published or disclosed, except as required by law. Breach of confidentiality is a disciplinary matter.

Retaliation against a complainant or witness, including victimisation, intimidation or adverse treatment, is prohibited and is treated as a separate violation under this policy and HR-POL-005.

### 7.1 False or malicious complaints

If the IC concludes that a complaint was knowingly false or malicious, or that evidence was forged, it may recommend action against the person concerned. **A complaint that cannot be proved, or that lacks sufficient evidence, is not a false complaint**; people should not be discouraged from complaining for fear of being unable to prove their case.

## 8. Awareness and training

- All employees complete POSH awareness training **within 30 days of joining** and **every year** after that (ONB-001). Contractors and interns complete it within 7 days of starting (ONB-005).
- IC members receive specialised training at least once a year.
- Managers receive additional guidance on recognising and responding to concerns.
- The IC prepares an **annual report** of complaints and actions, which the company submits to the District Officer as required by law and includes, in summary, in its annual compliance reporting.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| All employees | Treat others with respect; complete training; report or support complaints |
| Managers | Model respectful behaviour; take concerns seriously and direct people to the IC; never discourage complaints |
| Internal Committee | Receive complaints, conduct conciliation or inquiry, recommend action and interim relief |
| CHRO | Ensure ICs are constituted and trained; implement recommendations within 60 days |
| Facilities | Display IC details; support safety measures such as safe transport for late work (OPS-004) |
| Legal and Compliance | Advise the IC on procedure; file statutory reports |

## 10. Exceptions

There are no exceptions to this policy. The IC may, for good reason recorded in writing, extend timelines within the limits allowed by law.

## 11. Worked examples

**Example 1 - chat messages.** An employee repeatedly sends a colleague comments about her appearance in TeamTalk late at night, and she has asked him to stop. This is sexual harassment even though it happened online and outside office hours.

**Example 2 - time limit.** An incident occurred four months ago. The complainant explains she was on medical leave and afraid to complain. The IC may accept the complaint by extending the time limit by up to 3 further months.

**Example 3 - interim relief.** During an inquiry, the complainant reports to the respondent. On her written request, the IC recommends that the respondent stop supervising her work until the inquiry ends.

**Example 4 - customer behaviour.** A customer's manager makes sexual remarks to a Veltrix consultant during an on-site visit. The consultant complains to the IC; the company raises the matter with the customer and may withdraw staff from the site.

**Example 5 - unproved complaint.** After inquiry, the IC finds there is not enough evidence to prove a complaint. This does not make the complaint false, and no action is taken against the complainant.

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**Q: Does this policy protect men and non-binary employees?**
A: Yes. Veltrix extends the same protection and process to employees of all genders.

**Q: How long do I have to make a complaint?**
A: Within 3 months of the incident; the IC may extend this by another 3 months for good reason.

**Q: How long does the inquiry take?**
A: The IC completes it within 90 days of receiving the complaint.

**Q: How soon must the company act on the IC's recommendations?**
A: Within 60 days of receiving the report.

**Q: Can conciliation involve a payment?**
A: No. Monetary settlement cannot be part of conciliation.

**Q: When must I complete POSH training?**
A: Within 30 days of joining and every year after that.

**Q: Does harassment on a video call count?**
A: Yes. Remote work settings are part of the workplace.
