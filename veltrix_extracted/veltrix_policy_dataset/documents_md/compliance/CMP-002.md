# Conflict of Interest and Gifts Policy

## 1. Purpose

A conflict of interest arises when a personal interest could influence, or could appear to influence, the decisions a person makes for Veltrix. Conflicts are common and are not wrong in themselves; what matters is that they are declared and managed so that decisions remain fair and are seen to be fair. This policy explains what must be declared, how declarations are reviewed and managed, the rules on outside work and investments, and the rules on gifts and hospitality.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to all employees, directors and interns, and to contractors in respect of their work for Veltrix. It covers conflicts arising from family and personal relationships, financial interests, outside employment and business activities, external appointments, and gifts and hospitality.

## 3. Definitions

- **Relative:** spouse or partner, parents, children, siblings, parents-in-law, and the spouses of children and siblings.
- **Business partner:** any customer, prospective customer, vendor, reseller, implementation partner or competitor of Veltrix.
- **Material financial interest:** a shareholding of **more than 1%** in a business partner, or an investment in a business partner worth **more than Rs 5,00,000**, held by the employee or a relative. Investments through diversified mutual funds are excluded.
- **Declaration:** a written disclosure made in PeopleHub under *Declarations*.

## 4. What must be declared

Declare any of the following **within 30 days** of the situation arising, and confirm all declarations in the **annual declaration every April**:
1. A relative or close personal relationship with another employee where either person can influence the other's work, pay or career.
2. A relative who works for a business partner in a role that deals with Veltrix.
3. A material financial interest in a business partner.
4. Any outside employment, consulting, freelancing or business activity (section 6).
5. Any directorship, partnership or advisory role in another organisation, including non-profit boards.
6. Involvement, personally or through a relative, in any decision to select or pay a business partner in which you have an interest.
7. Any other situation that a reasonable colleague might see as influencing your judgement.

When in doubt, declare. A declaration that turns out to be unnecessary causes no harm; an undeclared conflict can lead to disciplinary action.

## 5. How declarations are managed

1. The employee submits the declaration in PeopleHub.
2. The reporting manager and the HR business partner review it within 10 working days. Declarations involving senior leaders (L7 and above) or business partners with contracts above Rs 25,00,000 are also reviewed by the Head of Legal and Compliance.
3. The reviewers decide whether a conflict exists and, if so, how it will be managed. Common measures are:
   - **recusal:** the employee takes no part in decisions involving the business partner or relative;
   - **reassignment:** responsibilities or reporting lines are changed;
   - **divestment:** the employee sells the financial interest within an agreed period;
   - **approval with conditions:** for example, outside work allowed outside working hours only.
4. The outcome is recorded in PeopleHub and shared with the employee in writing.

## 6. Outside work and business activities

Employees may take up outside work only with the **prior approval of their manager and HR business partner**, and only if:
- the work is not for a competitor, customer or vendor of Veltrix;
- it takes place outside working hours and does not affect performance or availability;
- it does not use company time, equipment, information or contacts;
- it does not create intellectual property in the same field as Veltrix products without written agreement from the CTO.

Teaching, writing, volunteering and personal hobby projects that are clearly unrelated to Veltrix's business normally need only a declaration, not prior approval. **External directorships of companies require CEO approval**; non-profit board roles require only a declaration.

## 7. Relationships at work

Relatives may work at Veltrix, but **a relative may not be in the reporting line of another relative**, and neither may take part in decisions on the other's hiring, pay, rating or promotion (see HR-POL-004). Close personal or romantic relationships between employees where one can influence the other's work must be declared; HR will arrange a change in reporting line where needed. Declarations are kept confidential and shared only with the people who need to manage the conflict.

## 8. Gifts and hospitality

### 8.1 Receiving

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Gifts from a business partner worth **up to Rs 3,000** | May be accepted; share perishable items with the team |
| Gifts worth more than Rs 3,000 | Politely decline, or hand to HR for the **gift register**; HR may donate the item to charity |
| Cash, vouchers, gift cards, loans | **Never** accepted |
| Business meals | Allowed if reasonable and occasional, with a business purpose |
| Travel or accommodation paid by a business partner | Only with department-head approval and for a clear business reason (for example a vendor's customer advisory board) |
| Any gift or hospitality during a live tender, renewal or negotiation with the giver | Not accepted |

### 8.2 Giving

Gifts to business partners must be modest and branded where possible. Gifts **up to Rs 3,000** need manager approval; **gifts above Rs 3,000 need department-head pre-approval** and an entry in the gift register. Cash and cash equivalents are never given. Anything given to a **public official** requires prior written approval from the Head of Legal and Compliance under the Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy (CMP-001). Reimbursement of gifts follows FIN-RMB-001.

### 8.3 The gift register

HR maintains the gift register, which records the date, giver or recipient, description, estimated value and decision for every gift above Rs 3,000 and every gift involving a public official. The register is reviewed quarterly by Legal and Compliance.

## 9. Use of company information and opportunities

Employees must not use confidential company information for personal gain, including buying or selling shares of a listed customer or partner based on non-public information. Business opportunities that come to an employee because of their role at Veltrix belong to Veltrix; employees may not pursue them personally without written approval from the CEO.

## 10. Procurement decisions

Anyone involved in selecting or paying vendors under the Procurement and Purchase Order Procedure (OPS-001) must declare any interest in a bidder before evaluation starts and must recuse themselves from the evaluation if a conflict exists. Procurement checks vendor ownership against declarations for contracts above Rs 25,00,000.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| All employees | Declare conflicts within 30 days and annually in April; follow gift rules |
| Reporting manager | Review declarations within 10 working days; apply agreed measures |
| HR business partner | Co-review declarations; maintain the gift register; arrange reporting-line changes |
| Head of Legal and Compliance | Review senior and high-value declarations; advise on complex cases; review the gift register quarterly |
| Procurement | Check vendor ownership against declarations |
| CEO | Approve external directorships and use of business opportunities |

## 12. Consequences

Failing to declare a conflict, accepting prohibited gifts, or using company information for personal gain is serious misconduct under HR-POL-005, and may be gross misconduct where it involves dishonesty or financial loss.

## 13. Exceptions

The Head of Legal and Compliance may approve exceptions to gift limits for cultural events or ceremonial occasions, provided they are recorded in the gift register. No exceptions are made for cash, vouchers or gifts during a live tender.

## 14. Worked examples

**Example 1 - relative at a vendor.** Deepa's brother is a sales manager at a company bidding for Veltrix's cloud contract. Deepa, who sits on the evaluation panel, declares the relationship and recuses herself from the evaluation.

**Example 2 - shareholding.** Manoj owns shares worth Rs 7,00,000 in a listed company that is a Veltrix reseller. This is above Rs 5,00,000, so it is a material financial interest that he must declare. His manager agrees he will not take part in reseller commission decisions.

**Example 3 - weekend freelancing.** A designer wants to design wedding invitations for friends at weekends. This is unrelated to Veltrix's business and needs only a declaration.

**Example 4 - expensive gift.** A vendor sends an employee headphones worth Rs 9,000 after a contract renewal. The employee hands them to HR for the gift register, because they exceed Rs 3,000.

**Example 5 - board seat.** A director-level employee is invited to join the board of a start-up. External directorships require CEO approval before acceptance.

## 15. Frequently asked questions

**Q: How soon must I declare a conflict of interest?**
A: Within 30 days of it arising, and again in the annual declaration every April.

**Q: What is a material financial interest?**
A: A shareholding above 1% in a business partner, or an investment in one worth more than Rs 5,00,000.

**Q: Can I accept a gift worth Rs 2,500 from a vendor?**
A: Yes. Gifts up to Rs 3,000 may be accepted, except during a live tender or negotiation with that vendor.

**Q: Can I work part-time for another company?**
A: Only with prior approval from your manager and HR business partner, and never for a competitor, customer or vendor.

**Q: Can my sister report to me?**
A: No. A relative may not be in the reporting line of another relative.

**Q: Who approves an external directorship?**
A: The CEO.

**Q: Who keeps the gift register?**
A: HR, with quarterly review by Legal and Compliance.
