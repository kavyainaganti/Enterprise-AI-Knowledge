# Performance Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

Performance management at Veltrix exists to help people do their best work, to recognise strong contributions fairly, and to address under-performance early and respectfully. This policy describes the review cycles, how goals are set, how ratings are decided and calibrated, how feedback is given, and what happens when performance does not meet expectations. It is designed to be predictable: every employee should know at the start of a cycle what is expected and how they will be assessed.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to all permanent employees. Employees who joined less than 3 months before the end of a cycle are not rated in that cycle; instead they receive a written check-in and are rated in the next cycle. Employees on probation are assessed through the probation review in ONB-004, not through this policy, until they are confirmed.

## 3. Principles

1. **Clarity:** goals are written down, measurable where possible and agreed at the start of the cycle.
2. **Continuous feedback:** the formal review summarises a cycle of regular conversations; it should contain no surprises.
3. **Evidence over impressions:** ratings are based on outcomes and observed behaviour, not on visibility or hours worked.
4. **Fairness:** ratings are calibrated across teams and checked for bias.
5. **Development:** every review ends with at least one development action.

## 4. Review cycles and timeline

Veltrix runs **two review cycles each year: April to September, and October to March**. Each cycle follows the same timeline.

| Week of cycle | Activity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1-2 | Goal setting: 3 to 5 goals agreed and entered in PeopleHub | Employee and manager |
| Month 3 | Mid-cycle check-in: progress, obstacles and support needed | Manager |
| Last 2 weeks | Self-review with evidence | Employee |
| Last 2 weeks | Manager review and proposed rating | Manager |
| Within 3 weeks after cycle end | Calibration meeting | Department head and HR business partner |
| Within 5 weeks after cycle end | Final rating shared in a one-to-one conversation | Manager |

The October-to-March cycle rating is used for the annual compensation review effective 1 April. Compensation guidelines that link ratings to salary increases are maintained by HR and are not part of this policy.

## 5. Goal setting

Goals should cover the main outcomes expected of the role, and at least one goal should relate to how the employee works with others or develops their skills. A good goal states what will be achieved, how it will be measured and by when. Goals can be changed during the cycle when priorities change; changes must be recorded in PeopleHub with the date and reason so that the final review assesses the employee against the goals that actually applied.

**Example of a weak goal:** "Improve dashboard performance."
**Example of a strong goal:** "Reduce average load time of the five most-used Veltrix Insight dashboards from 6 seconds to under 3 seconds by 30 September, measured in the monitoring tool."

## 6. Rating scale

| Rating | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Outstanding | Exceeded almost all goals and had impact well beyond the role |
| 4 | Exceeds expectations | Met all goals and exceeded several |
| 3 | Meets expectations | Met the goals of the role reliably; the expected rating for a solid performer |
| 2 | Partially meets expectations | Met some goals; clear gaps that need improvement |
| 1 | Does not meet expectations | Did not meet most goals or showed serious behavioural concerns |

A rating of 3 is a positive outcome. Managers should not treat it as average or disappointing.

## 7. Calibration

Calibration makes ratings comparable across managers. The HR business partner shares the draft distribution for each department two working days before the meeting. In the meeting, managers present evidence for every rating of 5, and for every rating of 1 or 2. The group checks for patterns that may indicate bias, for example differences by gender, office location or work pattern (such as employees who use more remote days). The company publishes a guide distribution, but it is **not a quota**: no employee's rating may be lowered simply to fit a distribution.

After calibration, ratings are locked in PeopleHub. **Any change after the lock requires CHRO approval** and a written reason.

## 8. Feedback conversations

The final rating must be shared in a live one-to-one conversation, in person or by video, before it becomes visible in PeopleHub. Managers should explain the rating with examples, acknowledge strengths, discuss gaps, and agree at least one development action. Employees may add written comments to the review record within 10 working days.

## 9. Under-performance and the performance improvement plan

### 9.1 When a PIP is used

A performance improvement plan (PIP) is used when an employee is rated **1**, or rated **2 in two consecutive cycles**. A manager may also propose a PIP mid-cycle where there is serious, documented under-performance, with HR business partner agreement.

### 9.2 How a PIP works

1. The manager and HR business partner agree 3 or 4 specific, measurable goals that can realistically be achieved.
2. The PIP lasts **60 days**. Weekly check-ins are held and recorded in PeopleHub.
3. The company provides the support identified in the plan, such as training, a mentor, or reduced scope.
4. At the end of the PIP, the outcome is one of: **successful** (PIP closed), **extended once by up to 30 days** where real progress has been made, or **unsuccessful**.
5. An unsuccessful PIP may lead to separation, handled under the Employee Conduct and Disciplinary Policy (HR-POL-005).

### 9.3 Safeguards

A PIP may not start while the employee is on approved leave of more than 2 weeks, or within 30 days of the employee raising a grievance, without CHRO review. Employees may be accompanied by a colleague of their choice in the PIP outcome meeting.

## 10. Recognition and promotion

Promotions are effective **1 April or 1 October**. A promotion requires a sustained rating of 4 or 5 or a clear case that the employee already works at the next level, a vacancy or business need at the next level, and approval by the department head and HR business partner. Promotion decisions are reviewed in the same calibration meeting as ratings. Spot recognition awards can be given at any time and do not depend on the review cycle.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Employee | Propose goals, ask for feedback, complete the self-review with evidence, own development actions |
| Manager | Agree goals, give regular feedback, write fair reviews, attend calibration, hold the rating conversation |
| Department head | Chair calibration, approve promotions, ensure consistency across teams |
| HR business partner | Facilitate calibration, check for bias, advise on PIPs |
| CHRO | Approve changes to locked ratings and policy exceptions |

## 12. Appeals

An employee who believes a rating is unfair may appeal in writing to the HR business partner within **10 working days** of the rating conversation. The department head reviews the evidence with the HR business partner and responds within 15 working days. The appeal outcome is final. Appeals that allege discrimination or harassment are handled under the relevant compliance policy instead.

## 13. Exceptions

Employees on long leave (for example maternity leave or long sick leave) for more than half of a cycle are not rated for that cycle and retain their previous rating for compensation purposes. Employees who change teams in the last 2 months of a cycle are rated by the new manager with written input from the previous manager.

## 14. Worked examples

**Example 1 - new joiner.** Nikhil joined on 15 August. The cycle ends on 30 September, less than 3 months later, so he is not rated in that cycle. He receives a written check-in and is rated at the end of March.

**Example 2 - PIP trigger.** Swathi was rated 2 in the April-September cycle and 2 again in the October-March cycle. Two consecutive ratings of 2 trigger a PIP.

**Example 3 - PIP extension.** At day 60, Ajay has met two of his three PIP goals and is close on the third. His manager and HR agree a single extension of 30 days. A further extension is not allowed.

**Example 4 - changing a locked rating.** After calibration, a manager realises that a major delivery was missed in the evidence for an employee. Because ratings are locked, the manager must submit the change with a written reason for CHRO approval.

## 15. Frequently asked questions

**Q: How many review cycles are there in a year?**
A: Two: April to September, and October to March.

**Q: How many goals should I have?**
A: Between 3 and 5 goals, agreed in the first two weeks of the cycle.

**Q: Is a rating of 3 bad?**
A: No. A 3 means you reliably met the goals of your role.

**Q: When is a performance improvement plan used?**
A: When an employee is rated 1, or rated 2 in two consecutive cycles.

**Q: How long does a PIP last?**
A: 60 days, with one possible extension of up to 30 days.

**Q: When are promotions effective?**
A: On 1 April or 1 October.

**Q: How do I appeal my rating?**
A: In writing to your HR business partner within 10 working days of the rating conversation.

**Q: Who can change a rating after calibration?**
A: Only the CHRO can approve a change after ratings are locked.
