# Employee Conduct and Disciplinary Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy describes the standards of behaviour Veltrix expects from everyone who works here, and the fair process the company follows when those standards are not met. Most conduct issues are resolved through a conversation. The formal process exists for the minority of cases where a conversation is not enough, and it is designed so that every employee knows the allegation against them, has a real opportunity to respond, and receives a decision that is proportionate.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to all employees, interns and fixed-term staff, at work, at company events, on business travel, and online where conduct is connected to the company (for example in TeamTalk channels or on social media when identifying as a Veltrix employee). Contractors are expected to meet the same standards; breaches by contractors are addressed with their supplier under ONB-005.

## 3. Standards of conduct

Every employee is expected to:
- act honestly and never falsify records, timesheets, expense claims, attendance or customer data;
- treat colleagues, candidates, customers and vendors with respect;
- follow company policies, including those on information security (IT-GDL-001), anti-bribery (CMP-001), conflicts of interest and gifts (CMP-002) and the prevention of sexual harassment (CMP-005);
- protect company and customer information;
- be fit for work and not under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs during working hours or at work events where they represent the company;
- attend work as agreed under the Hybrid Work and Attendance Policy (HR-POL-002) and the Leave and Holidays Policy (HR-POL-001);
- raise concerns through proper channels, including the Whistleblower and Speak-Up Policy (CMP-004).

### 3.1 Conduct in specific settings

**Company events and offsites.** The same standards apply at team dinners, offsites and the annual user conference. Where alcohol is served at a company event, employees are expected to drink responsibly; managers present at the event are responsible for ending it if behaviour becomes inappropriate, and the company arranges transport home for late-evening events.

**Online and social media.** Employees may mention that they work at Veltrix on personal profiles, but must make it clear that views are their own, must not share confidential information, and must not post content that harasses or demeans colleagues, customers or competitors. Official company channels are managed only by the Marketing team.

**Customer sites.** When working at a customer's premises, employees follow the customer's site rules in addition to this policy. If the two conflict, the stricter rule applies, and the employee informs their manager.

**Outside work.** Conduct outside work is a matter for this policy only where it has a clear link to the company, for example a criminal conviction relevant to the role, or public behaviour that identifies the employee as a Veltrix representative.

## 4. Misconduct categories

### 4.1 Minor misconduct

Examples: occasional lateness to meetings, repeated failure to mark remote days, careless use of company equipment, unprofessional tone in internal messages. Minor misconduct is normally handled informally by the manager in the first instance.

### 4.2 Serious misconduct

Examples: repeated minor misconduct after a warning, refusing a reasonable instruction, sharing Internal data outside the company, unauthorised absence of three or more consecutive working days, breach of the hybrid policy after a warning, inappropriate comments that fall short of harassment.

### 4.3 Gross misconduct

Gross misconduct is behaviour so serious that it may justify termination for a first offence. Examples include:
- fraud, theft or deliberate falsification of records or expense claims;
- offering or accepting bribes;
- sexual harassment, as determined by the Internal Committee under CMP-005;
- violence or threats of violence;
- deliberate disclosure or theft of Confidential or Restricted data;
- serious breach of information security, such as sharing passwords or disabling security controls deliberately;
- working under the influence of alcohol or drugs in a way that endangers others;
- retaliation against someone who raised a concern in good faith.

## 5. Informal resolution

For minor issues, the manager should speak privately with the employee as soon as possible, explain the concern, listen to the employee's view and agree what needs to change. The manager keeps a brief note of the conversation in PeopleHub under *Manager Notes*. This is not a disciplinary sanction and does not appear in the employee's formal record.

## 6. Formal disciplinary procedure

### 6.1 Investigation

Before any sanction beyond a verbal warning, HR appoints an investigator who has no reporting relationship with the employee or the complainant. The investigator gathers documents, interviews witnesses and the employee, and produces a written report with findings of fact, normally **within 30 days**. Cases involving sexual harassment are investigated by the Internal Committee under CMP-005, and cases involving fraud or bribery may be investigated by the Ethics Committee under CMP-004.

### 6.2 Suspension

Where the allegation is of gross misconduct, or where the employee's presence could hinder the investigation, the CHRO may place the employee on **paid suspension for up to 14 days**, extendable once by 14 days. Suspension is not a sanction and does not imply guilt. During suspension, system access may be restricted by IT on HR's instruction.

### 6.3 Notice of allegation and response

The employee receives a written notice describing the allegation and the evidence. The employee has **3 working days** to respond in writing, and may request a meeting. The employee may be accompanied at the meeting by a colleague of their choice.

### 6.4 Decision and sanctions

| Stage | Typically used for | Decided by |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal warning | First instance of minor misconduct that was not resolved informally | Manager with HR business partner |
| Written warning | Repeated minor misconduct or a first instance of serious misconduct | HR business partner |
| Final written warning | Further misconduct after a written warning, or very serious misconduct | Department head and HR head |
| Termination | Gross misconduct, or further misconduct after a final written warning | CHRO |

Warnings remain **active for 12 months** from the date of issue. An active warning is taken into account if further misconduct occurs. After 12 months the warning remains on file but is not considered in future decisions, except where the same type of serious misconduct recurs.

### 6.5 Appeal

The employee may appeal any formal sanction in writing **within 5 working days** of receiving the decision. The appeal is heard by a manager senior to the person who made the decision, with a different HR representative. The appeal decision is final within the company.

## 7. Absence procedure

When an employee is absent for **3 or more consecutive working days without approval or contact**, the manager and HR follow these steps:
1. Day 3: the manager calls and messages the employee; HR emails the personal email address on file.
2. Day 5: HR sends a written notice to the registered home address asking the employee to report or explain within 3 working days.
3. If there is no response, HR may treat the absence as serious misconduct and start the formal procedure.
Throughout, the company first checks for a genuine emergency, such as a medical crisis.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Employee | Meet the standards in section 3; co-operate with investigations; maintain confidentiality |
| Manager | Address issues early and informally; involve HR before any formal step |
| HR business partner | Advise managers; issue written warnings; ensure the process is followed |
| Investigator | Establish facts impartially and report within 30 days |
| Department head and HR head | Decide final written warnings |
| CHRO | Approve suspension and decide terminations |

## 9. Confidentiality and records

Disciplinary records are Restricted data. They are accessible only to HR, the decision-maker and, on appeal, the appeal manager. Witnesses are asked to keep the matter confidential. Records are retained in line with the Data Classification, Handling and Retention Guideline (IT-GDL-004).

## 10. Exceptions

The CHRO may vary the timelines in this policy where an employee is on medical leave, where witnesses are unavailable, or where a criminal investigation is in progress. Any variation is communicated to the employee in writing. The principles of fair notice and a chance to respond are never waived.

## 11. Worked examples

**Example 1 - informal resolution.** Kiran has missed marking remote days in PeopleHub three times in a month. His manager discusses it with him and notes the conversation. No formal warning is issued.

**Example 2 - written warning.** Two months after a verbal warning for repeated lateness to client calls, Tanvi misses another client call without notice. The HR business partner issues a written warning, active for 12 months.

**Example 3 - expense fraud.** An audit finds that an employee submitted the same hotel receipt twice. After investigation confirms the duplication was deliberate, it is treated as gross misconduct, and the CHRO decides on termination.

**Example 4 - appeal.** Rajesh receives a final written warning on Monday. He must submit any appeal by the following Monday (5 working days).

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**Q: How long does a warning stay active?**
A: 12 months from the date it is issued.

**Q: How long do I have to respond to an allegation?**
A: 3 working days from receiving the written notice.

**Q: Can I bring someone to a disciplinary meeting?**
A: Yes, a colleague of your choice.

**Q: How long do I have to appeal a sanction?**
A: 5 working days from receiving the decision.

**Q: Who decides on termination?**
A: The CHRO.

**Q: Does suspension mean I am guilty?**
A: No. Suspension is paid, temporary and not a sanction.

**Q: What happens if I am absent for 3 days without contacting anyone?**
A: Your manager and HR will try to reach you; if there is no response, the absence may be treated as serious misconduct.
