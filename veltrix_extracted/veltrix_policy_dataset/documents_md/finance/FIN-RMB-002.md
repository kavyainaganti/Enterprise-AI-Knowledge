# Business Travel Policy

## 1. Purpose

Business travel helps Veltrix win and serve customers, build teams across offices, and learn from the wider industry. It is also a significant cost and carries safety and security risks. This policy explains when travel is justified, how it is approved and booked, the class of travel and accommodation allowed, daily allowances, and the safety rules every traveller must follow.

## 2. Scope

The policy covers all travel on company business: customer visits, inter-office travel, conferences, training and events, in India and internationally. Commuting between home and the base office is not business travel. Expense claims for travel follow the general rules in the Expense Reimbursement Policy (FIN-RMB-001); this policy adds rules specific to travel.

## 3. Is the trip necessary?

Before requesting travel, consider whether a video call would achieve the same result. Travel is justified when face-to-face presence materially improves the outcome, for example a contract negotiation, an implementation kick-off, a workshop that needs a whiteboard, or a team event. Managers should challenge trips that could be replaced by a call, and combine trips to the same city where possible.

## 4. Approval

| Trip type | Approver |
|---|---|
| Domestic travel | Reporting manager |
| International travel | Department head |
| Travel by department heads | Their own reporting line (for example the CFO for Finance) |
| Any trip with total estimated cost above Rs 5,00,000 | As per the Delegation of Authority (OPS-002) |
| Business class travel | CFO, in addition to the above |

Approval is given in ExpenseDesk as a *Travel Request* before any booking is made. Bookings made without an approved travel request are not reimbursed except in an emergency approved retrospectively by the department head.

## 5. Booking

### 5.1 Use the Travel Desk

All flights, trains and hotels are booked through the **Travel Desk in ExpenseDesk**, which uses the company's travel agency and negotiated rates. Employees should not book directly unless the Travel Desk confirms that a better fare is available elsewhere.

### 5.2 Advance booking

- **Domestic travel must be requested at least 7 days in advance.**
- **International travel must be requested at least 14 days in advance**, longer if a visa is needed.
Late requests may be approved but the employee must state the reason; repeated late booking is reported to the department head.

### 5.3 Class of travel

| Mode | Standard |
|---|---|
| Domestic and international flights | **Economy class** |
| Business class | **Only for flights longer than 8 hours, with CFO approval** |
| Trains | AC 2-tier or AC chair car |
| Buses | AC sleeper or seater for overnight routes |
| Airport transfers | Taxi or ride-hailing, standard vehicle category |

Employees may use their own frequent-flyer accounts and keep personal loyalty points, provided this does not increase the fare.

## 6. Accommodation

Hotel limits are the same as in FIN-RMB-001: **Rs 6,000 per night in metro cities and Rs 4,000 per night in non-metro cities**, before taxes. The Travel Desk shows partner hotels within the limits. For international trips, the Travel Desk proposes hotels at the negotiated corporate rate for the city, and the department head approves the hotel with the trip.

For stays longer than 14 consecutive nights in one city, the Travel Desk books a serviced apartment, which is usually cheaper. Staying with friends or family is allowed, but no accommodation allowance is paid in place of a hotel.

## 7. Meals and daily allowances

### 7.1 Domestic

Meals while travelling are reimbursed at actual cost up to **Rs 1,500 per day**, with receipts for amounts above Rs 500. Meals provided by the hotel, the customer or the event are not claimed.

### 7.2 International

For international travel, a per diem of **USD 75 per day** covers meals, local transport within the city, tips and small incidentals. No receipts are needed for items covered by the per diem. The per diem is reduced by 30% for each day on which lunch and dinner are provided (for example at a conference), and is paid for the days of travel and stay as approved.

### 7.3 Visas, passports and health requirements

The company pays visa fees, visa agency charges and required travel vaccinations for approved international trips. HR issues invitation and employment confirmation letters for visa applications within 3 working days of request. Employees are responsible for keeping their own passport valid for at least 6 months beyond the planned return date; passport issue or renewal fees are not reimbursed. If a visa is refused, the Travel Desk cancels bookings and the cancellation fees are treated as business costs.

### 7.4 Ground transport at the destination

Within a city, use taxis or ride-hailing in the standard vehicle category, or public transport where it is safe and practical. Car rentals are allowed only when they are cheaper than taxis for the trip, or when travelling to sites without reliable taxi service, and must be approved with the trip. Self-driven rentals abroad require a valid international driving permit, and fuel is reimbursed against receipts.

## 8. Travel advance and corporate cards

Employees who travel more than 4 times a year may hold a corporate card under FIN-RMB-005. Other employees may request a travel advance of up to 75% of the estimated trip cost; advances must be settled within 15 days of return. Details are in FIN-RMB-005.

## 9. Changes and cancellations

Changes are made through the Travel Desk. Cancellation and change fees are reimbursed when the change was caused by business reasons or by illness. Fees caused by personal choices (such as extending a trip for a holiday) are paid by the employee.

### 9.1 Combining business and personal travel

Employees may add personal days to a business trip, provided the extra days are taken as leave under HR-POL-001, the company's cost does not increase, and the personal portion is booked and paid by the employee. If a fare with personal days is cheaper than the business-only fare, the cheaper fare may be used.

## 10. Safety and security

- Every international trip is **registered with the Travel Desk**, which records contact details and the itinerary so the company can reach travellers in an emergency (see OPS-003).
- Travel insurance is provided automatically with every booking; carry the insurance card.
- Keep the company laptop in hand luggage and never leave it unattended; report loss within 1 hour (IT-GDL-001).
- Use the company VPN on hotel and airport networks.
- Before travelling to a country on the company's high-risk list, obtain approval from the CISO (device security) and the CHRO (personal safety); travellers may be issued a separate loaner laptop.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Traveller | Request approval early, book through the Travel Desk, follow limits and safety rules, submit claims within 30 days |
| Reporting manager | Approve domestic trips; challenge unnecessary travel |
| Department head | Approve international trips and exceptions |
| CFO | Approve business class travel |
| Travel Desk | Book travel at negotiated rates; register trips; support travellers in emergencies |
| Finance Operations | Check travel claims; reconcile advances and card spend |

## 12. Exceptions

Exceptions to class of travel or hotel limits must be approved in advance: up to 25% above a hotel limit by the department head; anything else by the Finance Controller, except business class, which always needs the CFO. Medical needs (for example extra legroom for a back condition) are approved by the department head on the advice of HR.

## 13. Worked examples

**Example 1 - international approval.** Pranav is invited to a partner conference in Singapore. He submits a travel request 20 days before departure. His department head approves it. The flight is about 5.5 hours, so economy class applies.

**Example 2 - long-haul flight.** Shreya flies to a customer in Frankfurt, a flight of more than 8 hours. Business class is allowed only if the CFO approves in addition to her department head.

**Example 3 - per diem with conference meals.** On two of Amit's four days in Dubai, lunch and dinner are provided by the conference. He receives USD 75 for two days and USD 52.50 (USD 75 reduced by 30%) for the other two days.

**Example 4 - adding a holiday.** Rina extends a Goa business trip by two days of earned leave. The return fare on the later date is Rs 800 more. She pays the Rs 800 difference and her own hotel for the extra nights.

**Example 5 - late domestic booking.** A customer asks for an urgent meeting in Chennai in three days. The manager approves a late request with the reason "customer escalation"; the 7-day rule is waived for this trip.

## 14. Frequently asked questions

**Q: How far in advance must I request domestic travel?**
A: At least 7 days in advance.

**Q: And international travel?**
A: At least 14 days in advance, longer if you need a visa.

**Q: Who approves international travel?**
A: Your department head.

**Q: Can I fly business class?**
A: Only for flights longer than 8 hours, and only with CFO approval.

**Q: What is the international per diem?**
A: USD 75 per day, reduced by 30% for days on which lunch and dinner are provided.

**Q: Can I keep my airline loyalty points?**
A: Yes, if using your account does not increase the fare.

**Q: Do I need to register my international trip?**
A: Yes, with the Travel Desk, so the company can reach you in an emergency.

**Q: Can I add personal days to a business trip?**
A: Yes, as leave, provided the company's cost does not increase.
