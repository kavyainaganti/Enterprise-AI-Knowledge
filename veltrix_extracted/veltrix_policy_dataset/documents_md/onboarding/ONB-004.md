# Probation and Confirmation Policy

## 1. Purpose

Probation is a period at the start of employment in which the employee and the company confirm that the role is a good fit. It gives the employee time to learn the role with support, and gives the company a structured opportunity to assess performance and conduct before confirming employment. This policy sets the length of probation, how progress is reviewed, the possible outcomes, and the rights of the employee throughout.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to all new permanent employees at every grade. It does not apply to:
- employees moving to a new role through internal transfer or promotion;
- former employees re-hired within 12 months of leaving, who return directly as confirmed employees;
- interns and contractors, who are covered by their engagement terms (ONB-005).
Fixed-term employees on contracts of less than 12 months do not serve probation; those on longer contracts serve probation as described here.

Part-time employees serve the same 6-month probation measured in calendar time, but managers should set goals that reflect their contracted hours, and the 30, 60 and 90-day check-ins may be combined where the role allows.

## 3. Length of probation

Probation lasts **6 months** from the date of joining. It may be **extended once, by up to 3 months**, under section 6.2. The total probation period can therefore never exceed 9 months.

If an employee is absent for more than 15 working days in total during probation (for example because of extended sick leave), the probation period is extended by the length of the absence beyond 15 working days, so that the employee has a fair amount of working time to demonstrate their abilities. Such an extension does not count as the single discretionary extension in section 6.2.

## 4. Support during probation

Every employee on probation receives:
- a structured onboarding programme with 30, 60 and 90-day check-ins (ONB-001);
- an onboarding buddy;
- clear goals recorded in PeopleHub;
- weekly one-to-ones with the manager;
- access to learning resources relevant to the role.

The performance review cycle described in HR-POL-003 does not apply until the employee is confirmed; probation reviews replace it.

## 5. Reviews during probation

| Review | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 30-day check-in | Around day 30 | Settling in and early goals |
| 60-day check-in | Around day 60 | Progress and support needed |
| 90-day review | Around day 90 | Written mid-probation review |
| Month-4 checkpoint | End of month 4 | Last point for documenting concerns that could affect confirmation |
| Confirmation review | **2 weeks before probation ends** | Manager's recommendation |

Any concern that may lead to an extension or ending of probation must be discussed with the employee and **documented in PeopleHub by the end of month 4**, with a support plan. Concerns raised for the first time at the confirmation review cannot by themselves justify ending employment, unless they concern serious misconduct that occurred after month 4.

## 6. Outcomes

### 6.1 Confirmation

When the employee has met the expectations of the role, the manager recommends confirmation in PeopleHub at the confirmation review. The HR business partner reviews the recommendation, and HR issues a **confirmation letter within 5 working days** of the decision. On confirmation:
- the notice period changes to the standard notice period for the grade (60 days for L1-L4 and 90 days for L5 and above);
- the employee becomes eligible for the one-time home office allowance under the Hybrid Work and Attendance Policy (HR-POL-002) and FIN-RMB-001;
- the limit on earned leave use during probation no longer applies (HR-POL-001).

### 6.2 Extension

Probation may be extended once, by up to 3 months, when the employee has made progress but has not yet fully met expectations, and the concerns were documented by month 4. The extension must be approved by the department head and HR business partner and communicated in writing **before the original probation end date**, with specific goals for the extension period.

### 6.3 Ending employment during probation

If the employee has not met expectations despite documented support, or after an unsuccessful extension, employment may end. The decision is approved by the department head and the HR business partner, and by the CHRO for grades L5 and above. Before a decision, the employee is told the concerns in writing and has **3 working days** to respond. Ending employment for gross misconduct follows HR-POL-005 at any stage.

### 6.4 No decision recorded

PeopleHub alerts the manager and HR business partner when the confirmation review is due. If no outcome has been recorded by the probation end date, HR escalates to the department head. **If no decision is recorded within 10 working days after the end date, the employee is deemed confirmed**, and HR issues the confirmation letter.

### 6.5 Communicating outcomes

All probation outcomes are communicated by the manager in a live conversation, with the HR business partner present for extensions and endings, before the written letter is issued. For an extension, the manager explains what has gone well, what still needs to improve, the specific goals for the extension period and the support that will be provided. For an ending, the employee is told the reasons, the notice arrangements and the exit steps, and is offered a meeting with HR to discuss final pay, leave encashment and the return of equipment. Outcomes are kept confidential; colleagues are told only that the person is leaving, unless the employee agrees to share more.

## 7. Notice during probation

During probation, either the employee or the company may end employment by giving **15 days' written notice**, or pay in lieu of notice. After confirmation the standard notice periods apply.

## 8. Leave and benefits during probation

Employees on probation receive full salary and benefits from day 1, including health insurance. Earned leave accrues from the first month, but **no more than 6 days of earned leave may be used during probation** without CHRO approval; casual and sick leave can be used normally (HR-POL-001).

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Employee | Engage with goals and feedback, ask for support, complete mandatory training |
| Manager | Set goals, hold check-ins, document concerns by month 4, recommend an outcome 2 weeks before the end |
| HR business partner | Review recommendations, ensure fairness and consistency, approve extensions and endings with the department head |
| Department head | Approve extensions and endings |
| CHRO | Approve endings for L5 and above; approve exceptions |
| HR Operations | Issue confirmation and extension letters, update PeopleHub |

## 10. Exceptions

The CHRO may shorten probation for senior hires with exceptional track records, or waive it for employees joining through an acquisition. Shortened probation must be stated in the offer letter. Any variation from this policy must be in writing.

## 11. Worked examples

**Example 1 - standard confirmation.** Anjali joins on 6 April. Her probation ends on 5 October. Her manager completes the confirmation review in the week of 21 September, two weeks before the end date, and HR issues her confirmation letter within 5 working days.

**Example 2 - extension.** Rohan's manager documented concerns about the quality of his reports at the end of month 3 and agreed a support plan. At the confirmation review, Rohan has improved but is not yet consistent. The department head and HR business partner approve a 2-month extension, communicated in writing before his original end date.

**Example 3 - undocumented concern.** In the final month of probation, a manager raises concerns about an employee's communication for the first time. Because nothing was documented by month 4, the employee cannot be refused confirmation on this basis alone; the manager should address it through normal feedback after confirmation.

**Example 4 - long sick leave.** Smita is absent for 25 working days during probation after surgery. Her probation is extended by 10 working days (the absence beyond 15 days). This is not counted as the single discretionary extension.

**Example 5 - no decision.** A manager is on leave and nobody records an outcome for Tarun by his end date. HR escalates; if no decision is recorded within 10 working days after the end date, Tarun is deemed confirmed.

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**Q: How long is probation?**
A: 6 months from the date of joining.

**Q: Can probation be extended?**
A: Yes, once, by up to 3 months, so the maximum is 9 months.

**Q: What is the notice period during probation?**
A: 15 days' written notice from either side.

**Q: When does my notice period become 60 or 90 days?**
A: On confirmation: 60 days for L1-L4 and 90 days for L5 and above.

**Q: How much earned leave can I use during probation?**
A: Up to 6 days without CHRO approval.

**Q: What happens if my manager forgets to complete my confirmation?**
A: HR escalates; if no decision is recorded within 10 working days after your end date, you are deemed confirmed.

**Q: Do I serve probation again if I am promoted?**
A: No. Probation does not apply to internal promotions or transfers.
