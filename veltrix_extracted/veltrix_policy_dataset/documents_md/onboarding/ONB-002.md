# Manager's Onboarding Responsibilities Guide

## 1. Purpose

A new employee's manager has more influence on their first months than anyone else. This guide tells managers exactly what to do, and when, so that every new team member gets a consistent, high-quality start. It complements the New Employee Onboarding Programme (ONB-001), which is written for joiners, and links to the probation process (ONB-004). It is restricted to people managers and HR because it includes guidance on assessing and addressing early performance concerns.

## 2. Scope

The guide applies to every manager who receives a new permanent or fixed-term employee, whether hired externally, transferred from another team, or returning from long leave into a new role. Managers of interns and contractors should use the shorter checklist in ONB-005.

## 3. Why it matters

Veltrix tracks two onboarding measures for every department: the **new-joiner survey score** at 30 and 90 days (target **4 out of 5 or higher**) and **early attrition**, meaning joiners who leave within their first 6 months. Departments with early attrition above 10% in a year review their onboarding with their HR business partner. Most early exits are linked to unclear expectations, missing tools or lack of manager time in the first weeks, all of which this guide addresses.

## 4. Manager checklist

### 4.1 As soon as the offer is accepted

- [ ] Confirm the joining date with Talent Acquisition; joining days are Mondays (ONB-001).
- [ ] Send a short personal welcome message.
- [ ] Decide which role-specific systems the joiner will need beyond the default access.
- [ ] Inform the team of the new joiner's name, role and start date.

### 4.2 At least 5 working days before joining

- [ ] Raise **HelpPoint access requests for role-specific systems** (for example LeadLoop, TrackWise projects, finance systems), so that IT can complete approvals before day 1. Default accounts are created automatically 2 working days before joining (ONB-003).
- [ ] Request additional equipment (second monitor, headset) through HelpPoint.
- [ ] Confirm a desk with Facilities if the joiner will be office-based on day 1.

### 4.3 At least 1 week before joining

- [ ] **Assign an onboarding buddy** from the team or a neighbouring team. Choose someone with at least 12 months of service, good knowledge of how things work, and time to help. Do not assign yourself.
- [ ] Prepare a first-week schedule, including introductions to key colleagues outside the team.
- [ ] Draft 30, 60 and 90-day goals.

### 4.4 Day 1

- [ ] Block at least two hours of your own time: a morning meeting and an end-of-day wrap-up.
- [ ] Explain the role, how it connects to team and company goals, and how the team works (meetings, anchor days, tools).
- [ ] Share the first-week schedule.
- [ ] Introduce the buddy and the team.

### 4.5 Week 1

- [ ] Agree the 30, 60 and 90-day goals and record them in PeopleHub.
- [ ] Check that the joiner has all access needed; escalate missing access through HelpPoint.
- [ ] Hold a short daily check-in.

## 5. Setting early goals

Early goals should balance learning and contribution. A useful pattern is:
- **By day 30:** understands the team's work, products and key processes; has completed mandatory training; has delivered one small piece of real work.
- **By day 60:** handles routine tasks independently; has contributed to a larger piece of work with guidance.
- **By day 90:** owns core responsibilities; can explain priorities for the rest of probation.

Goals should be specific to the role. For a product manager, a day-60 goal might be "write and get approval for one feature specification"; for a finance analyst, "prepare the month-end accrual schedule for one cost centre with review".

## 6. Check-ins and reviews

| Check-in | Timing | Focus | Record in PeopleHub? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly one-to-one | Throughout probation | Progress, blockers, feedback | Brief notes |
| 30-day check-in | Around day 30 | Settling in, access, early goals | Yes |
| 60-day check-in | Around day 60 | Skills, independence, relationships | Yes |
| 90-day review | Around day 90 | Ownership, goals for rest of probation, early feedback on confirmation | Yes |
| Confirmation review | 2 weeks before probation ends | Recommendation to confirm, extend or end (ONB-004) | Yes |

Manager notes must be factual and professional. Employees can request access to their records, and notes may be relevant evidence in any later decision.

### 6.1 Giving useful feedback to new joiners

New joiners often hesitate to ask for feedback because they do not want to appear unsure. Make feedback a normal part of every one-to-one rather than an event. Good feedback in the first months:
- is **timely**, given within a day or two of the work it relates to;
- is **specific**, describing what was done and its effect ("the summary you wrote for the customer was clear and they approved it without changes");
- separates **learning gaps** (things nobody has explained yet) from **performance gaps** (things the joiner knows but does not do);
- ends with a clear next step.

Ask the joiner for feedback too: what has been confusing, what would have helped them start faster, and whether they have the access and information they need. Share recurring themes with the HR business partner so the onboarding programme can improve.

## 7. Addressing early concerns

If you have concerns about a new employee's performance or conduct:
1. Raise them **early and specifically** in a one-to-one, with examples.
2. Agree what needs to change and what support you will provide.
3. Record the conversation in PeopleHub.
4. Inform your HR business partner.

**Concerns that could lead to an extension or ending of probation must be documented by the end of month 4.** Raising them for the first time at the confirmation review is unfair to the employee and makes it difficult to justify a decision. Probation outcomes are described in ONB-004; the performance review process in HR-POL-003 does not apply until confirmation.

## 8. Remote and hybrid joiners

For remote joiners, confirm with IT that the laptop has been couriered to arrive before day 1 and schedule the day-1 set-up by video. Plan at least one in-person team visit during the first 60 days where practical; travel is booked under the Business Travel Policy. For hybrid joiners, align the joiner's office days with team anchor days during the first month so they can learn from colleagues in person.

## 9. Internal transfers

When an employee joins your team from another team, there is no new-joiner programme, but you should still: agree new goals within 2 weeks, request any new access and confirm that access from the previous role is removed or re-approved (IT-GDL-002), introduce key contacts, and hold a 30-day check-in.

## 10. New managers

Employees who become managers for the first time, whether by promotion or hiring, complete the *Management Essentials* learning path within 90 days of taking on the role. The path covers one-to-ones, goal setting, feedback, performance management, recruitment and the legal basics of managing people. It is funded centrally and does not use the individual learning budget (FIN-RMB-004).

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Manager | Complete the checklist, set goals, hold check-ins, address concerns early, recommend probation outcome |
| Buddy | Practical support and introductions; weekly check-in during the first month |
| HR business partner | Coach managers, review survey and early-attrition data, advise on concerns |
| IT Service Desk | Provision default and requested access before day 1 |
| Facilities | Desk, ID card and badge on day 1 |

## 12. Exceptions

Where a manager is on leave when a joiner starts, the manager must nominate a deputy manager who completes the day-1 and week-1 steps. Where a new team is being formed and no buddy with 12 months of service is available, the HR business partner may assign a buddy from another department.

## 13. Worked examples

**Example 1 - access before day 1.** A new sales engineer joins on 14 September. The manager raises the LeadLoop access request on 3 September, more than 5 working days earlier, so approvals are complete before day 1.

**Example 2 - late concern.** In month 5, a manager decides to recommend extending a joiner's probation but has no recorded concerns. The HR business partner advises that, because concerns were not documented by month 4, the manager should first hold a documented conversation and support plan before recommending an extension.

**Example 3 - buddy choice.** A manager plans to buddy a new hire with an employee who joined 7 months ago. Because buddies should have at least 12 months of service, the manager chooses a different colleague.

## 14. Frequently asked questions

**Q: When should I request role-specific access for a new joiner?**
A: At least 5 working days before the joining date, through HelpPoint.

**Q: When must I assign a buddy?**
A: At least one week before the joining date.

**Q: Can I be the buddy myself?**
A: No. The buddy must be someone other than the manager.

**Q: By when must probation concerns be documented?**
A: By the end of month 4.

**Q: What is the target new-joiner survey score?**
A: 4 out of 5 or higher.

**Q: What is early attrition?**
A: Joiners who leave within their first 6 months.

**Q: What training do new managers complete?**
A: The Management Essentials learning path, within 90 days of taking on the role.
